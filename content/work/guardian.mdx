## Problem

A founder can ship a site that leaks customer data and never find out. Security
review means hiring someone, which is the first line a startup cuts, and by the
time it is affordable the data has usually already gone somewhere.

The compliance half is duller and more expensive to ignore. Zimbabwe's Cyber and
Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07] puts real obligations on any business
processing personal data, and S.I. 155 of 2024 requires a data controller
licence from POTRAZ, registration once you handle the personal data of fifty or
more people, and a named data protection officer whose details are meant to be
published. POTRAZ can audit, fine, and issue corrective orders. Most founders
have never read any of it.

None of that is helped by another tool that reports CVE numbers at a founder who
cannot read them. A founder needs three things: what is wrong, whether it
matters for a business their size, and what to change today.

## Process

The scan runs against the public surface of a site, because that is what a
founder can act on without commissioning a server-side engagement. Findings come
back ranked by severity, and each one states the risk in plain terms before it
states the technical detail. A founder who cannot read a finding will not act on
it, so the wording is treated as part of the feature rather than as copy.

The compliance mapping is what makes it useful locally rather than generic. A
finding is tied to the specific obligation it touches under Chapter 12:07, so
the output reads as a list of things the business is required to do, not as a
list of things a tool dislikes. That is the difference between a report a
founder acts on and one they close.

The interface is built in plain CSS with no framework, on the same reasoning:
it has to load fast on a cheap connection and stay readable when the person
running it is not technical.
